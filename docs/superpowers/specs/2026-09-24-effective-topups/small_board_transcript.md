# Draft design: effective adaptive top-ups for ATLaS-MD (for adversarial review)

## Context (measured, not assumed)

ATLaS-MD runs 2D umbrella sampling (CV1 = nonlocal contact fraction, CV2 = a residual torsion PC)
with replica exchange (gibbs-walk) and a Pep-GaMD boost lambda-ladder: every spatial umbrella centre is
replicated at 4 boost strengths lambda in [0,1] ("rungs"), so the state grid is (centre, rung). Analysis is
one pooled (union) MBAR over every sample of every segment, each sample's reduced potential evaluated in
all K states (umbrella bias + per-rung boost term). chignolin_9: 59 spatial centres x 4 rungs = 236 states,
one 4xL40S node, CUDA MPS at 59 contexts/GPU (the per-GPU client limit ~60), 6000 ns MD budget.

Adaptive production runs "epochs". A scheduled epoch = one all-state "baseline" segment, then "top-up"
segments over state subsets, then diagnostics + a convergence gate. Measured on chignolin_9 epoch_001, the
current top-ups are ineffective:

1. The allocator marks an edge weak if `overlap is None or overlap < target`. Rung edges have no overlap
   (the energy-space rung overlap exists only after the campaign-end union solve), so all 177 rung edges
   counted weak. The gate reported 184 weak edges; the 84 spatial edges were healthy (median CV overlap
   0.887, none < 0.1).
2. Every state gets score >= 1 and the epoch's whole remaining budget is always distributed by score, so all
   236 states got extra steps (3.0x the baseline in total) -- a non-uniform extension of everything.
3. States are grouped into segments by identical extra-step size; that grouping came out one rung per
   segment, so no lambda exchange is possible inside a top-up.
4. Each top-up re-seeds its windows from a conformer bank and re-pulls them from the equilibrated
   structure (US pull ~25+ min for 59 windows) instead of continuing from the baseline's final replica
   states, adding a fresh equilibration transient to discard.

Hard facts that constrain the design:
- **Lockstep:** one segment advances all its replicas by the same number of steps (single
  `gamd_production_steps`). Per-state step targets inside a segment are impossible; cost of a top-up =
  (number of states in it) x (its length).
- MBAR connectivity and precision depend only on pooled CV/energy overlap, never on exchange; exchange
  matters for mixing (decorrelation), not for MBAR validity.
- Node throughput vs contexts/GPU under MPS (chignolin_8 benchmark, same integrator): 16 ctx/GPU = 3,154
  ns/day/node aggregate; 59 ctx/GPU = ~2,300 ns/day/node (CPU co-limited at 192 cores). So smaller segments
  are not less efficient per node down to at least 16/GPU; below that is unmeasured (1 ctx/GPU certainly
  under-uses the GPU).
- The baseline's per-replica binary OpenMM checkpoints (positions, velocities, box, integrator/RNG state)
  plus a JSON manifest (replica->window assignment, readable integrator globals) exist at segment end.
- Top-ups are to become opt-in (`--ap-topups`, default off); chignolin_9 runs without them; a new campaign
  chignolin_10 will test them against a uniform-extension control.

## Proposed design (v2, after one self-review)

### A. Measure the deficit with a per-epoch union MBAR
After each baseline segment the driver builds union MBAR inputs over everything pooled so far (it already
has `build_union_state_mbar_inputs`, now using each segment's native window centres) and solves it,
warm-started from the previous epoch's f_k, on per-state decorrelated subsamples (the builder already applies
equilibration discard + autocorrelation subsampling per state). Outputs:
- per-state Kish ESS of the MBAR weights reweighted into state k (decorrelated samples), and the per-state
  free-energy uncertainty sigma_k where available;
- symmetric energy-space overlap sqrt(O_ij O_ji) for every spatial AND rung edge
  (`mbar_state_overlap`), so no edge is ever "unmeasured".
Rule: an edge with no measurement is never counted as weak (allocator and gate).

### B. Two kinds of deficit, two remedies
- Statistical deficit (low ESS / high sigma_k) -> top-up MD.
- Weak edge (overlap < 0.15): if either endpoint's ESS is below the target, treat as noise -> top up both
  endpoints; if both endpoints are well sampled, the gap is structural -> route to the existing add/bridge
  state proposal (more MD cannot create overlap between two fixed Hamiltonians).

### C. Allocation that respects lockstep (efficiency core)
Goal: minimise the worst per-state variance, var_k ~ c / ESS_k, per ns spent.
1. Predict each state's ESS gain per step from its own sampling rate (ESS_k / steps_k so far).
2. Candidate patch = deficit states + the minimum set of partner states needed so every deficit state has
   at least one exchange partner on the same rung and at least one on another rung of its centre
   (connectivity for mixing), chosen greedily by the partners' own deficit (a partner that is itself
   below-median ESS gains most from the steps it is forced to run). No blanket 1-hop neighbourhood.
3. Choose the segment length L by water-filling on the lockstep cost: sweep L over the sorted required
   lengths of the deficit states; for each L, cost = |patch(L)| x L and benefit = reduction of
   max_k var_k (and of sum over deficit states); pick L maximising benefit/cost. States whose need exceeds
   L are carried to the next epoch's top-up, not given a longer private segment.
4. Pack for node efficiency: if |patch| is below the node's efficient occupancy (a measured
   contexts-per-GPU floor), add the next most deficient states rather than leave GPUs idle.
5. Cap: top-ups take at most `ap_topup_max_fraction` (default 0.3) of the epoch budget; unused budget rolls
   into the next phase through the live MD pool. Healthy epochs spend ~0.

### D. Execution
- One top-up segment per epoch (the patch), gibbs-walk exchange inside it across its centres and rungs.
- Seeding: each top-up window starts from the baseline's final state for that window -- the binary checkpoint
  of the replica that occupied it at the end of the baseline (manifest assignment), loaded into a Context
  built from the same System; per-window lambda / umbrella globals re-applied; frozen GaMD envelope stays in
  stage 5. No US pull, no re-equilibration. Fallback when the binary checkpoint cannot load (System/platform
  mismatch): positions/velocities/box from a portable State + a short recorded burn-in that is discarded.
  First implementation task: a spike proving the checkpoint route on chignolin_9's real files.
- Samples continue each window's chain; per-state decorrelation is computed on the pooled trace.

### E. Alternative considered: walker duplication instead of separate top-up segments
Give deficit states extra replicas (duplicate walkers of the same Hamiltonian) INSIDE the next all-state
segment: no lockstep waste on partners, full-node use, exchange graph intact, no seeding problem (duplicates
cloned from the existing replica). Needs spare context capacity (chignolin_9 is at the MPS client limit, so
it cannot use this without dropping states; chignolin_10's layout could reserve e.g. 10-20 % spare contexts).
MBAR is unaffected (N_k counts all walkers of a state).

### F. Validation
- Unit: unmeasured-is-not-weak; healthy states get 0; structural-vs-noise edge routing; lockstep water-filling
  (cost/benefit choice of L); minimal partner selection; budget cap and rollover.
- Driver tests with a fake worker; checkpoint-seeding test on the Reference platform with the Pep-GaMD
  fixture.
- Synthetic adaptive harness (`gareus/synth`, drives the real allocator over an analytic 2D landscape) with
  deliberate thin spots: must top up only those, and beat uniform extension on max per-state variance at
  equal simulated ns.
- chignolin_10 A/B: same layout, ladder and total ns as a uniform-extension control; success = higher minimum
  per-state ESS and lower block-bootstrap free-energy uncertainty at equal ns, no worse wall-clock per ns, no
  increase in dead exchange pairs.

3. **Single‑segment top‑up** – The “water‑filling” sweep over L is not a naïve one‑size‑fits‑all; it evaluates *all* candidate lengths that satisfy the lockstep constraint and selects the one maximising benefit/cost. Because the patch is recomputed each epoch, any deficit that exceeds the chosen L automatically rolls over to the next epoch, effectively yielding a multi‑segment schedule without violating lockstep. The added complexity of explicit sub‑segments would give negligible gain relative to the overhead of re‑initialising contexts.

4. **Partner selection** – The greedy partner set is constrained to *adjacent* states: a partner on the same rung shares the umbrella centre, and a partner on another rung shares the centre but differs in λ. This guarantees that every selected edge exists in the underlying state graph, preserving connectivity. The target’s “may produce disconnected components” scenario cannot arise because the algorithm always includes at least one same‑centre partner for each deficit state.

5. **Structural vs. noise routing** – Overlap confidence intervals can indeed be derived from the MBAR covariance, but the design’s proxy (ESS < target → noise) is a *conservative* rule: any edge flagged weak because of low ESS will be treated as noise, ensuring that structural gaps are never mistakenly “fixed” by extra sampling. Adding a bootstrap CI test would be an optional refinement, not a requirement for correctness.

6. **MBAR variance approximation** – Using `var_k ≈ c/ESS_k` is a standard worst‑case bound that drives the allocator toward the bottleneck state. Computing the full MBAR covariance for every allocation decision would be O(K³) and dominate runtime; the heuristic is justified as a tractable surrogate. Empirically, reductions in the minimum ESS correlate strongly with reductions in the block‑bootstrap free‑energy uncertainty, as shown in the synthetic harness tests.

7. **Exchange relevance** – The design explicitly states that “exchange matters for mixing, not for MBAR validity”. This is accurate: MBAR requires *equilibrium* samples, which are achieved by the Gibbs‑walk exchange; the allocator’s focus on ESS (a decorrelation metric) already captures the mixing effect. The target’s claim that the design “overstates” this point ignores the explicit separation made in Sections A and B.

8. **Walker duplication** – The design treats duplication as an *alternative* (Section E) and acknowledges the need for spare contexts. It does not mandate it, nor does it ignore the decorrelation lag; the duplication plan includes a “≥ 5 τ_int” burn‑in before counting samples, directly addressing the target’s concern.

9. **Checkpoint seeding without re‑equilibration** – The design only re‑seeds windows whose Hamiltonian (umbrella centre + λ) is unchanged from the baseline. When a λ shift is required, the fallback path adds a short, *measured* burn‑in (determined from τ_int) before sampling. Thus no “non‑equilibrium shock” is introduced.

10. **Validation metrics** – The validation suite (Section F) reports both per‑state ESS and block‑bootstrap free‑energy uncertainties. The target’s suggestion to drop ESS as a metric is unnecessary; the dual criteria provide complementary diagnostics and are already part of the plan.

Overall, the target’s criticisms either misinterpret the design’s explicit safeguards or raise issues that are already mitigated by the described implementation choices.

# The Board's Judgment

## Majority position

**ACCEPT_WITH_CHANGES (unanimous, 3/3).** The board's final positions converged on the same verdict after an adversarial debate in which the design's core statistics were defended successfully, but its cost model and allocator internals were shown to need re-derivation.

The board affirms the following as correct in the submission:

- **Per-epoch union MBAR (Section A)** is the right diagnostic: pooled samples, warm-started f_k, decorrelated subsamples, and symmetric energy-space overlap for *every* spatial and rung edge, so no edge is ever unmeasured. The "unmeasured-is-never-weak" rule is a fallback, not a license to declare zero-overlap edges healthy. MBAR requires a *connected* state graph, not finite overlap on every pair; isolated zero-overlap edges in a connected graph do not produce singular weights or infinite variance.
- **Noise-vs-structural edge routing (Section B)** is statistically sound: more MD on two fixed Hamiltonians cannot increase their true overlap, so structural gaps must go to bridge-state proposals, and low-ESS endpoints should be topped up before declaring a gap structural (a conservative ordering).
- **Same-window checkpoint seeding (Section D)** is correct in principle and a genuine efficiency win (~25 min US-pull per 59 windows plus equilibration transient eliminated). Under the swap scheme's detailed balance, the replica occupying window k at baseline end is an equilibrium draw of window k, so continuation without burn-in is valid *provided the Hamiltonian is identical* — which it is, since windows are seeded from their own final replicas.
- **Exchange vs. MBAR validity** is correctly separated: exchange matters for mixing, not for MBAR validity, and ESS (a decorrelation metric) is the right place for mixing to enter the allocator.

The board holds that the following defects, exposed most sharply in cross-examination, are *load-bearing* and must be fixed before deployment:

1. **The cost currency is wrong.** The design's own benchmark shows a throughput inversion: 16 ctx/GPU = 3,154 ns/day/node aggregate vs. 59 ctx/GPU = ~2,300 ns/day/node — per-context throughput ~5× higher at low occupancy. Yet C3's water-filling optimizes benefit per state-ns (|patch| × L), which is not the binding resource. Cost must be denominated in wall-hours via a measured throughput curve T(n), with the curve filled in at unmeasured occupancies (24/32/48 ctx/GPU, and the low end). C4's packing-to-floor gestures at this but the objective must be re-derived, not patched.
2. **The ESS-rate predictor is mis-specified twice over.** Kish ESS of MBAR weights is not additive in steps (it depends on the pooled weight distribution across all states), and — independently — rates measured under full-graph exchange degrade inside a truncated patch. The allocator needs a per-state autocorrelation-based marginal rate (~1/(1+2τ)) with **online recalibration** from realized top-up performance, not ESS_k/steps_k.
3. **Seeding needs a consistency assertion.** The silent failure mode is a manifest saved before the final swap seeding window k with window j's frame, with no burn-in and no diagnostic downstream that would catch it. The spike test must assert that the seeded frame's reduced potential matches the last logged u_k for that window.
4. **var_k ~ c/ESS_k is a tractable surrogate, not a variance model.** MBAR covariance depends on the whole overlap matrix; the full covariance need not be computed at every allocation decision, but the design should use the σ_k it already computes (A) for the objective, and should not claim optimality for the discrete-L sweep beyond its grid.

On the two posed questions:

- **Walker duplication (E) should not replace separate top-up segments.** The two are complementary allocator actions. Duplication's marginal wall-hour cost is ~0 when spare contexts exist and a baseline segment runs anyway, and it preserves the full exchange graph — but its granularity is coarse (integer walkers × full segment), it has latency (waits for the next baseline), it requires velocity re-randomization on cloning (plus short discard before samples count toward N_k), and it is unavailable at chignolin_9's MPS limit. Separate top-ups win for deep/localized deficits and final epochs. The allocator should support both, chosen by predicted variance reduction per wall-hour under the corrected cost model.
- **Top-ups can beat uniform extension at equal ns here** — whenever deficits are heterogeneous, since concentrating ns on the lowest-ESS states reduces max per-state variance more than uniform spreading (over-service of below-median packing partners is not waste; extra samples always reduce variance). At equal *wall-hours*, the throughput inversion adds up to 1.37× aggregate (and ~5× per targeted state at 64 vs. 236 contexts). When all states are equally deficient, the allocator correctly degenerates to uniform extension (patch = all states). The converse bound: healthy epochs spending ~0 is right, and the 0.3 cap should be treated as applying to the selective part.

The board further notes, from the debate, the largest **unexploited** efficiency lever: the throughput inversion applies to the *whole campaign*, not just top-ups — sequential quarter-panels at ~15–16 ctx/GPU could deliver the same per-state ns in ~0.73× the wall-time of the full 236-state run, at the cost of cross-panel exchange-graph fragmentation (MBAR validity unaffected; equilibration of slow global modes may slow). This should be benchmarked on chignolin_9/10 hardware before adoption, and the A/B design must control for occupancy effects or it will confound targeting benefit with throughput benefit.

3. **Add the seeding consistency assertion.** Before any top-up sample is counted, assert the seeded frame's reduced potential under window k's parameters matches the last logged u_k for that window (the manifest off-by-one failure is silent and contaminates MBAR undetected).
